Getting the Most From Your Loaf

Bread Care

A few minutes in the oven brings a loaf right back to life — and if it's a day or two past its best, it still has plenty left to give.

Section One

Reviving a Loaf

Because there are no preservatives in my bread, it dries out faster than store-bought loaves — that's a good thing! A quick trip under the tap and into a hot oven brings back that just-baked crust and soft interior.

01
Run It Under Water
Hold the whole loaf briefly under running water — just enough to wet the crust all over, not soak it through. This steam is what re-crisps the crust and re-hydrates the crumb.
02
Preheat the Oven
Set your oven to 425–450°F. Place the wetted loaf directly on the rack or on a baking sheet — no need to wrap it.
03
Bake 10–15 Minutes
Bake until the crust is crackling and golden again and the loaf feels warm all the way through. Smaller items like a half loaf or bagels will be closer to 10 minutes; a full whole loaf closer to 15.
04
Rest, Then Slice
Let it sit 5 minutes before slicing — straight out of the oven the crumb is still setting, and a short rest keeps it from gumming up under the knife.

Section Two

Give It a Second Life

Bread that's a few days old is a gift, not a problem — some of the best kitchen staples start with a loaf that isn't at its freshest. Here are a few of my favorite ways to use it up.

Croutons
Cube the bread, toss with olive oil, salt, and herbs, and toast in the oven until golden and crisp. Perfect on soups and salads.
Breadcrumbs
Pulse dried or lightly toasted bread in a food processor. Freeze in a bag and pull out a handful anytime a recipe calls for them.
French Toast
Slightly stale bread is actually ideal here — it soaks up the egg custard without falling apart, giving you a custardy center and golden edges.
Bread Pudding
Torn or cubed loaf, baked with eggs, milk, and a little sugar and cinnamon, turns day-old bread into a warm, comforting dessert.
Panzanella
A classic Italian bread salad — torn chunks tossed with ripe tomatoes, olive oil, vinegar, and fresh basil. The bread soaks up all the juices.
Simply Toasted
Don't overlook the obvious — a good toaster or a hot skillet with a little butter revives most slices in under two minutes.

A note on freshness: because there's nothing in my bread to slow staling, it's best enjoyed within 2–3 days at room temperature, wrapped in a paper or cloth bag rather than sealed in plastic. For longer storage, slice and freeze — toast straight from frozen anytime.
